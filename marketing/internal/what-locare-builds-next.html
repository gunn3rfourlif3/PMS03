What Locare builds next

Four things we're considering. Before writing any of them we'd rather hear from someone who actually runs a rental book — which of these is real, and which is us guessing.

Arrears

The tenant who is always a little short

What happens now

A tenant owes R8,000 and pays R7,500. Every month. They're never really in arrears, so nobody escalates. Eight months later they owe R4,000 — and because each payment clears the oldest invoice, your age analysis has said "about a month behind" the entire time.

What the age analysis shows 30 days
What they actually owe R4,000

What we'd build

Locare watches the balance and the pattern rather than the age, and opens a case when it crosses a line you set — half a month's rent, short three months in six, two failed debit orders. It tells you why in one sentence: "R4,312, 0.54× the rent, short in 8 of the last 8 months."

It also counts your notice periods in business days, with public holidays, and puts any case whose deadline has passed at the top of the list — because a breach notice nobody acted on is a process you have to start again.

And if there's a maintenance job that's been open on that unit since before the short payments started, it says so before you send a letter of demand.

What we want to know

  • Do you have tenants like this right now? How many, roughly?
  • How do you currently track that a notice period has run out?
  • Has a missed deadline ever cost you months on an eviction?
Inspections

Eight inspections, one sensible route

What happens now

Inspections get booked as tenants answer their phones, so a Tuesday can run Randburg → Kempton Park → Randburg again. The driving is invisible: it never appears as a cost, it just eats the day.

What we'd build

Locare groups everything due in a period by area and proposes a day's work in a sensible order — "Tuesday: these six, roughly 34 km" — then hands it to the agent's phone and opens it in Google Maps. We're not building a navigation app; we're building the plan that should exist before anyone gets in the car.

What we want to know

  • How do you plan a day of inspections at the moment — diary, spreadsheet, memory?
  • Would you let the system choose the order, or does the tenant's availability always win?
  • Is the same true of contractor call-outs, or is that different?
Load-shedding

Which of your properties are dark this afternoon

What happens now

An agent arrives for a 14:00 inspection in a complex on Stage 4, and the gate motor, the pressure pump and the lights are all off. Or a tenant reports "no water" that is really a pump with no power.

What we'd build

Each property carries its own schedule, so you can see at a glance what's off and when before you book a visit — and answer a tenant's call without looking anything up.

Honest caveat: this is only worth building if it changes what you actually do. It may just be interesting.

What we want to know

  • Has load-shedding actually wasted a trip for you, or is it noise?
  • Do tenants call you about it, and does that cost you time worth money?
Landlords

Something to show a landlord you're pitching

What happens now

Landlords ask the same three questions — is my rent in, what came off it, what is happening with my tenant — and each one costs somebody a phone call or a hand-built statement.

What we'd build

A clean view your landlords sign into: rent received, what was deducted and why, maintenance and what it cost, lease status and when it expires. In your agency's name and colours, not ours.

The real point is the next mandate. When you're sitting across from a landlord who has four units with someone else, this is the thing you open on your laptop.

What we want to know

  • What do landlords ask you for most often?
  • When you've lost a mandate, what was the reason you were given?
  • Would you actually show this in a pitch, or is it a nice-to-have after signing?
Three questions, if you have ten minutes
  1. Which one would you want first? And roughly what would it be worth per month to you?
  2. Which would you not use? Saying so saves us weeks, and we'd rather hear it now.

3. What eats the most time in your week that isn't on this page at all?

This is the one we most want answered. Everything above came from watching one agency; the thing we've missed is probably obvious to you and invisible to us.

None of this is built yet and we're not promising dates. This page exists to find out what's worth building before we build the wrong thing.
