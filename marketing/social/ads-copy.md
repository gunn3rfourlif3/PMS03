# Paid social — post copy

Three concepts, 1080 × 1350 (4:5). Sources: `ad-a-ledger.html`,
`ad-b-whitelabel.html`, `ad-c-monthend.html`. Render with
`node shoot-ads.mjs`.

A note on what is deliberately absent. No customer count, no "trusted by N
agencies", no testimonial, no logo wall, no certification badge. One agency is
live and there are no paying customers yet, so every one of those would be a
claim we cannot make. The creatives lean on the product being specific instead,
which is the only honest lever available and — with a competitor set that all
says "all-in-one" — probably the stronger one anyway.

Every figure shown is illustrative and labelled as such. No real agency, owner,
tenant or unit appears in any creative.

---

## A — "Every cent, accounted for."

The trust-account concept. Aim it at agency principals and financial managers,
and at anyone who has ever been through an audit.

> **Every cent, accounted for.**
>
> Ask a rental agency principal what worries them and it is rarely the
> listings. It is the trust account.
>
> Locare posts every movement twice — rent received, commission taken, owner
> paid — on a ledger that cannot be edited. A correction is a new entry, never a
> quiet overwrite. So when someone asks how a number got there, the working is
> still on the screen.
>
> Property management software built for South African rental agencies.
> locare.co.za

## B — "Your brand on it. Never ours."

The white-label concept. Aim it at agencies who have been burned by software
that puts its own name in front of their clients, and at anyone building a brand
they intend to sell one day.

> **Your brand on it. Never ours.**
>
> Your tenants sign in at your domain. Your owners open a statement with your
> logo on it. Your colours, your name, your agency.
>
> Locare runs underneath — rent collection, trust accounting, owner payouts,
> maintenance — and stays out of sight. The only place our name appears is on
> the invoice we send you.
>
> White-label property management, built for South African agencies.
> locare.co.za

## C — "Month-end is a report. Not a reconstruction."

The month-end concept. The widest reach of the three, because the pain is
universal and needs no explaining. Good top-of-funnel; retarget into A or B.

> **Month-end is a report. Not a reconstruction.**
>
> A tenant's rent arrives. It is receipted into trust, the commission posts to
> income, the owner's statement issues in the same run, and the payout goes out.
> Nobody retypes anything into a spreadsheet.
>
> That is the whole idea: by the time you go looking for the number, it is
> already there.
>
> locare.co.za

## Running them

**Hashtags — use three, not twelve.** A wall of them reads as someone trying to
be found rather than someone worth finding, which is the opposite of what these
creatives are doing. `#PropertyManagement #RentalAgency #PropTech` is enough.

**Suggested test.** C is the widest net, so run it as the cold-audience opener.
A and B are for people who already know what the category is: retarget them at
anyone who watched, clicked or visited. A converts the finance-minded, B the
brand-minded — the split in results is itself worth knowing.

**Primary text is the ad's first line, not the image's.** The headline is in
the creative already; repeating it verbatim as the opening line of the post
wastes the strongest position in the ad. The copy above opens differently on
purpose.

**The landing page has to keep the promise.** An ad about a trust ledger that
lands on a generic homepage loses the thread. Send A and C to whatever page
talks about accounting; send B to whatever page talks about branding.
