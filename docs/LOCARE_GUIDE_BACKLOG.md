## House rules for a Locare guide

These are what separate a guide worth publishing from filler. A draft that fails
any of them should not be committed.

**Written for a principal, not for a search engine.** The reader runs a letting
agency and has a specific problem this week. If a paragraph would not survive
being read aloud to one, cut it.

**Specific to South Africa, and legally careful.** Name the actual statute —
Rental Housing Act, PPRA, POPIA, PIE — and the actual deadlines. Where the law
is unsettled or varies by province, say so rather than flattening it. Never give
legal advice; describe the obligation and say when to involve an attorney.

**No invented numbers.** Fees, interest rates, deadlines and thresholds are
checkable, and a wrong one in a guide is worse than no guide. If a figure cannot
be verified, write around it.

**Locare appears once, at the end, and only where it honestly fits.** A guide
that is a product pitch does not get read or linked to. The trust-audit guide is
the model: 1,200 words of genuinely useful procedure, then one paragraph.

**1,000–1,500 words.** Long enough to be the best answer on the page, short
enough to finish.
